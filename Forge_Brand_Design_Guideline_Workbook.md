**1. BRAND FOUNDATION & STRATEGY**

*Every strong brand starts with a clear understanding of who you are,
why you exist, and what makes you different. This section lays the
strategic groundwork for all design decisions that follow.*

**Company Overview**

  --------------------------- -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Company Name**            Forge
  **Industry / Sector**       Construction Technology (ConTech) / SaaS
  **Year Founded**            2026
  **Website (if existing)**   forgeequipment.com (primary) \| usedforge.com (under consideration) \| forge-web-seven-psi.vercel.app (staging)
  **Business Type**           B2B --- Serving general contractors, project managers, and construction firms
  --------------------------- -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Brand Story**

**▶** What inspired the creation of this company? What problem are you
solving?

> Contractors waste 20--40 minutes after every job walk juggling 4+
> tools (Meta Glasses, Otter.ai, ChatGPT, Buildertrend) just to organize
> notes, reformat scope, and hand off data to their PM. Critical details
> get lost in translation, formatting is inconsistent, and the process
> is entirely manual. Forge was built to eliminate that post-walk chaos
> --- turning a single job walk into structured, export-ready data with
> zero typing.

**▶** What is the origin story of the brand? Any meaningful history or
founder story?

> Born from firsthand frustration on real job sites. The founding team
> saw contractors repeatedly losing time and accuracy between the field
> and the office. The name "Forge" represents taking raw, unstructured
> field data and shaping it into something solid and useful --- like
> metal through a forge.

**6. COLOR PALETTE**

*Color is one of the most powerful tools in brand design. It evokes
emotions, establishes recognition, and creates visual hierarchy. This
section will define your entire color system.*

**Color Preferences**

**▶** Are there any colors you're drawn to for your brand? Any colors to
avoid?

> Client's vision is unmistakably dark-mode-first. Deep blacks, dark
> navy, and cyan/teal accents dominate. The aesthetic references sci-fi
> interfaces, premium tech dashboards, and field intelligence systems.
> Avoid: bright primary colors, pastels, warm oranges/reds as primaries.
> The palette should feel like a command center, not a children's app.

**Color System**

Derived from the client's vision board and current landing page:

  ----------------------- --------------------------------------------------------------------------
  **Primary Color 1**     \#0A0A0F --- Deep Black (primary background)
  **Primary Color 2**     \#0EA5E9 --- Cyan / Electric Blue (primary accent, CTAs, highlights)
  **Secondary Color 1**   \#1E293B --- Dark Slate (cards, elevated surfaces)
  **Secondary Color 2**   \#14B8A6 --- Teal (secondary accent, success states, data visualization)
  **Accent Color**        \#F8FAFC --- Near-White (primary text on dark backgrounds)
  **Dark Neutral**        \#111827 --- Charcoal (section backgrounds, footers)
  **Light Neutral**       \#94A3B8 --- Slate Gray (secondary text, labels, metadata)
  **Background Color**    \#050507 --- True Dark (body background)
  ----------------------- --------------------------------------------------------------------------

**Color Usage Rules**

**▶** Describe any specific rules for where colors should or shouldn't
be used:

> Cyan (\#0EA5E9) is reserved for primary CTAs, interactive elements,
> and key data highlights --- never used for large background fills.
> Teal (\#14B8A6) is for secondary UI elements, success states, and data
> visualization accents. Body text should always be near-white
> (\#F8FAFC) or slate gray (\#94A3B8) on dark backgrounds. The deep
> black background creates the premium, high-contrast foundation ---
> never use medium grays as page backgrounds.

**Accessibility**

All text colors must meet WCAG 2.1 AA contrast standards (minimum 4.5:1
for body text, 3:1 for large text). Document contrast ratios for your
primary combinations.

  ---------------------------------- -------------------------------------------------------
  **Primary text on background**     \#F8FAFC on \#0A0A0F --- Contrast ratio: 18.4:1 (AAA)
  **Secondary text on background**   \#94A3B8 on \#0A0A0F --- Contrast ratio: 7.1:1 (AA)
  **Link/accent on background**      \#0EA5E9 on \#0A0A0F --- Contrast ratio: 5.8:1 (AA)
  ---------------------------------- -------------------------------------------------------

**10. LAYOUT & GRID SYSTEMS**

*Consistent layout rules ensure your brand looks polished and
intentional across every format --- from a business card to a
billboard.*

**Grid Preferences**

  ----------------------------- ------------------------------------------------------------
  **Web Grid System**           12-column grid with max-width container (1280px)
  **Base Spacing Unit**         8px --- all spacing values are multiples of 8
  **Margin Standard (Web)**     Horizontal: 24px (mobile) / 48px (tablet) / 80px (desktop)
  **Margin Standard (Print)**   0.75in minimum --- 1in for formal documents
  ----------------------------- ------------------------------------------------------------

**Alignment & Spacing Rules**

**▶** Any specific preferences for how content should be aligned and
spaced?

> Center-aligned hero sections. Left-aligned body content within a
> contained max-width. Generous vertical whitespace between sections
> (120--160px) to let each section breathe. Feature sections alternate
> between left-text/right-visual and full-width centered layouts. Cards
> and UI elements use 24px internal padding with 16px gaps between
> items.

General principle: maintain consistent whitespace ratios. Elements
should breathe. Crowded layouts dilute your brand.
